# Project 5 Learning Guide

## What You Need To Understand

SOC 2 readiness is about preparing the company to prove its controls. It is not the same as passing an audit, and it is not a framework version called "SOC 2 2026." The relevant criteria are the AICPA Trust Services Criteria, with revised points of focus from 2022.

## The Secret Sauce

Many candidates say:

> I understand SOC 2.

A stronger answer is:

> I understand that SOC 2 readiness depends on control ownership, evidence quality, operating period, exception handling, remediation, and whether the company can prove controls operated consistently.

That answer shows you understand the actual work.

## Core Concepts

### PBC List

PBC means "prepared by client." It is the list of evidence the company must provide to auditors.

Examples:

- MFA configuration export
- Access-review evidence
- Vulnerability scan reports
- Security training completion report
- Incident-response plan
- Tabletop exercise evidence
- Backup restoration test
- Vendor review evidence

### Evidence Owner

Every evidence request needs an owner. If nobody owns it, it will be late or incomplete.

### Audit Period

SOC 2 Type II looks at whether controls operated over a period of time. A screenshot from one day may not prove consistent operation.

### Exception

An exception is where the control did not operate as expected or cannot be evidenced.

Example:

> Two users in the sample did not have MFA evidence.

### Remediation

Remediation is the corrective action, owner, target date, and validation method.

## Where You Should Learn Deeply

### Evidence Is Not Just a File

Strong answer:

> I would check whether the evidence covers the correct population, period, control objective, approval, and exceptions.

### Missing Evidence Can Be a Finding

Strong answer:

> If a control happened but cannot be evidenced, it may still create audit risk. I would ask for alternate evidence but also open a remediation item to improve future evidence retention.

### Readiness Happens Before The Audit

Strong answer:

> Readiness work is valuable because it gives the company time to fix control and evidence gaps before the formal audit period begins.

## Practice Exercise

Pick one PBC item and answer:

1. Which control does this evidence support?
2. Who owns the evidence?
3. What period does it cover?
4. What population does it cover?
5. What would make it incomplete?
6. What exception could appear?
7. What remediation would you recommend?
